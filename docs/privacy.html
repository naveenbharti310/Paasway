Passway privacy notice

Last updated: 17 October 2026

This notice describes the Passway Android app provided by LIMERENCE TECHNOLOGIES PRIVATE LIMITED. For privacy questions or requests, contact Limerencetechnologies@gmail.com.

Account information

Passway processes your phone number and one time authentication codes to sign you in. It uses Firebase Authentication and the 2Factor SMS service. The app stores an account identifier, your chosen name, account and age eligibility status, and a hashed representation of your phone number for safety and abuse prevention. A phone hash is pseudonymous information, not anonymous information. Your sign in phone number is not displayed to people you connect with. Information you choose to type in a chat can still reveal your identity.

When you accept the Passway use rules, the account profile records the rules version and the time of acceptance. You may decline and sign out or delete your account without accepting.

The app asks for your date of birth to check that you are at least 18. The adult account profile records the eligibility result, not the full date of birth. For an underage attempt, an eligibility hold can record when the person becomes eligible, which can imply their birth date.

Location and pairing

When you generate or enter a pairing code, Passway requests foreground location permission. Your device coordinates are sent to the service to check that the two people are nearby. The pairing code record stores a location cell, approximately 150 metres in size, rather than raw coordinates. This is still location data and is not anonymous. The app does not display your coordinates or location cell to the other person. Without location permission, the nearby pairing feature cannot work.

Chats, private notes and reports

The service processes messages, replies, connection records, private notes, block records, report reasons and associated timestamps. A connected person can see the messages you send and your chosen name. Private notes are scoped to their author and are not displayed to the other person. Messages are processed on the server; Passway does not offer end to end encryption. HTTPS protects data in transit.

Automated safety rules inspect message content. When the Google text moderation provider is enabled, message content is also sent to Google Cloud Natural Language for classification. These checks are not a guarantee that harmful messages will be detected or removed. A report can preserve the conversation for safety review.

Notifications and technical information

If you enable notifications, the app stores a push notification token and uses Expo and the platform notification service. Notification payloads contain event and connection identifiers and a generic event description, not message text or names. Service requests also involve IP addresses, authentication tokens, app or device verification information and operational logs. The service uses hashed IP and phone based rate limiting to help prevent abuse.

Internal diagnostic builds can send crash and technical performance diagnostics through Firebase Crashlytics. The public release configuration turns diagnostic collection off at app startup. Authentication and app verification services can still process technical device information needed for security. This is not a promise that every platform service collects no technical data.

Service providers and access

Passway uses Google Firebase for authentication and data storage, Google app verification services, Vercel for API hosting, 2Factor for SMS authentication, and Expo for push delivery. Google Cloud Natural Language can process message text for safety classification as described above. Providers process the information needed for those services under their own applicable terms and privacy notices. Phone numbers sent to Google for authentication may also be processed by Google to improve spam and abuse prevention. Information may be processed in countries other than where you live, depending on these providers' infrastructure.

Relevant provider notices: Google, Vercel, Expo, and 2Factor. Contact support if you need further information about a provider's role.

Passway's current app does not include advertising or in app purchases. This notice does not describe independent actions taken by a person who receives your messages, such as copying, photographing or sharing them.

Retention and deletion

  • Pairing codes stop being usable after five minutes or when cancelled or consumed. Expired records are cleaned up asynchronously.
  • Ordinary messages stop being available in the app after 72 hours. Physical deletion is asynchronous and can occur later. A reported conversation and its report can be retained for a 90 day safety window or an existing longer retention deadline; later reports can extend that deadline.
  • Private notes are removed when the associated connection is ended or the account is deleted.
  • Reported phone safety holds can remain for 180 days. Banned phone holds and block records have no automatic expiry. Underage holds can remain until the person reaches the minimum age.
  • After account deletion completes, a minimal old account identifier and phone hash tombstone can remain for seven days to prevent reuse of cached credentials. Deletion jobs and service cleanup can retry after outages. Provider operational and security records may follow the provider's own retention rules.

Account deletion removes the sign in account and profile, ends open connections and removes private notes. It does not immediately erase already sent messages or override safety related retention. See the account deletion instructions for both in app deletion and requests without app access.

Your choices

You can edit your displayed name, manage location and notification permissions in Android settings, end connections, block or report another account, and request deletion. If you need access to, correction of, or deletion of information that cannot be managed in the app, contact support. Account ownership may need to be verified before processing a request. Do not send an SMS verification code, password or banking information by email.

Passway is intended for people aged 18 and over. If you believe an underage person has an account, contact support. Material changes to data handling will require an updated notice; this page identifies its latest update date.
